Trajectory Optimization for Autonomous Drone Systems (PIMM Lab)

Research-Oriented Engineering Project — Supervised by Prof. Francisco Chinesta — 2026

Semester-long research project conducted at PIMM Laboratory under the supervision of Professor Francisco Chinesta, focused on energy-aware trajectory optimization for autonomous drones operating in dynamic and multi-agent environments. The project consists in formulating and implementing a variational optimization framework based on Euler–Lagrange equations to compute optimal trajectories minimizing a custom energy-driven cost function integrating velocity, acceleration, battery discharge dynamics, aerodynamic drag, and collision avoidance constraints. The mathematical model is translated into a full Python-based simulation framework, including 2D dynamic simulations, obstacle repulsion fields, and multi-agent interactions. The long-term objective is the construction of a hybrid digital twin of the drone system, combining physics-based modeling with physics-informed neural networks to approximate complex cost functions and enable real-time adaptive planning.
