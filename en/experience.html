Trajectory Optimization for Autonomous Drone Systems — PIMM Lab × CNRS@CREATE

Research Project at PIMM Lab, Paris · Research Internship at CNRS@CREATE, Singapore (June–August 2026)

Initiated in Paris at the PIMM Laboratory under the supervision of Professor Francisco Chinesta, this research project develops collision-aware trajectory optimization methods for autonomous systems operating around fixed and moving obstacles. From June to August 2026, the work continued through a research internship at CNRS@CREATE in Singapore, with technical supervision from Amine Ammar. The approach combines a variational planner based on the Euler–Lagrange equations with a causal, Newtonian dynamics-based corrector that adapts the nominal path to observed obstacles and agents. Developed first in Python simulation, the complete planning–perception–control chain was then transferred to an instrumented rover platform in Singapore, using camera calibration, ArUco tracking, and differential-wheel control to measure trajectory accuracy, clearance, and repeatability. The experimental campaign validated fixed-obstacle avoidance in both directions and continuous trajectory tracking, while moving-agent and multi-agent extensions remain under development. These theoretical, numerical, and experimental results are currently being consolidated into a research publication in preparation, with the longer-term objective of transferring the framework from ground-based validation to autonomous drone systems.
