01 · PROBLEM & OBJECTIVE

Demystifying the "Toe-Kick": Sports Intuition vs Impact Mechanics

In ball sports and particularly football, an entrenched belief among athletes holds that striking the ball with the toe ("le pointu") yields superior departure speed ("more pure power"), whereas the inside of the foot ("le plat du pied") guarantees precision. This competitive CPGE TIPE research project investigated whether contact geometry alone can intrinsically alter ball launch velocity.

To investigate this question, I developed an experimental and computational approach focused on isolating impact mechanics from human motor execution. Drawing on research in football impact physics conducted at LadHyX / École Polytechnique, I designed and built a repeatable pendulum-based test bench, implemented a high-speed video tracking workflow, automated kinematic data processing in Python, and developed a simplified dynamic model validated against experimental measurements.

02 · EXPERIMENTAL RIG & PROTOTYPING

Custom Pendulum Rig Design & Precision Prototyping

To eliminate biases inherent to human motor variability (fluctuating foot speeds, muscular deceleration, parasitic ankle flexion), I engineered a mechanical leg based on a compound pendulum weighing m₁ = 1.4 kg with length L = 46 cm, guided inside a 50 cm vertical structure. Interchangeable impactor heads were modeled and 3D printed in PLA: three cylinders of diameters 2r = 6 mm, 12 mm, and 24 mm (representing toe-kick geometries at different scales) as well as a wide flat plate (representing the inside of the foot). An elastic spherical ball inflated to controlled overpressure (p = 0.2 bar / 3 psi) was struck equatorially to isolate pure collision dynamics before introducing real football damping.

03 · ACQUISITION PIPELINE & PYTHON

Trajectory Tracking, Measurement Uncertainty, and Regressions

Each strike was recorded with high-frame-rate lateral video under calibrated lighting. To extract quantitative metrics, I developed an automated Python pipeline: coordinates of the impactor x₁(t) and ball center x₂(t) were tracked frame-by-frame across 50 to 120 ms intervals. Spatial calibration was set at 1 pixel = 0.00027 m. A rigorous Type B experimental uncertainty analysis (accounting for motion blur spots of 22 to 50 pixels, yielding u = 2Δ / √3) was applied to every data point. Ordinary least squares regressions (`scipy.stats.linregress`) isolated pre-impact striker speed (V₁), post-impact striker speed (V'₁), and ball launch speed (V'₂), while delimiting the contact zone.

Measured Velocityv'₂ = 4.0 m/s

At pendulum approach speed v₁ = 2.2 m/s, the ball departs at v'₂ = 4.0 m/s. The measured ratio v'₂ / v₁ ≈ 1.82 - 2.0 directly validates the heavy-striker elastic regime (m₁ ≫ m₂) and theoretical momentum conservation without major energy dissipation.

04 · DYNAMIC MODELING & FINDINGS

Two Formal Discoveries: Invariant Velocity, Modulated Contact Duration

The theoretical collision analysis is governed by linear momentum conservation over the brief impact duration (τ ≈ 10-20 ms). With external forces negligible over this window, m₁ v₁ + m₂ v₂ = m₁ v'₁ + m₂ v'₂. Introducing the coefficient of restitution e = -(v'₂ - v'₁) / (v₂ - v₁) and an initial stationary ball (v₂ = 0), the striking efficiency ratio evaluates analytically to:

v'₂ / v₁ = (1 + e) / (1 + m₂/m₁) In the heavy striker limit (m₁ ≫ m₂ ⇒ m₂/m₁ ≈ 0.05) and elastic impact (e ≈ 1), the ratio asymptotically reaches 2. Experimental results reveal two fundamental insights:

Summary of Physics Conclusions

  • 1. Exit Velocity Invariance: At equal striker approach speed V₁, the ball leaves the rig at the identical velocity V'₂ (4.0 m/s), regardless of whether the head is a 6 mm, 12 mm, 24 mm cylinder or a wide flat plate. There is no intrinsic "toe-kick effect" on exit speed in pure impact mechanics.
  • 2. Contact Duration Modulation: A wide contact surface provides a stiffer equivalent contact spring k, shortening interaction time (τ ≈ 10 ms). A small surface easily initiates deep localized deformation, producing a longer contact duration (τ ≈ 20 ms).
05 · FROM LAB TO PITCH: BIOMECHANICS

Disentangling Ball Physics from Human Striking Biomechanics

Why then do football players universally perceive a toe kick as inherently more powerful? Comparing pendulum measurements to human kicking kinematics and official standards reveals that the paradox stems entirely from joint biomechanics and proprioception:

Real-world losses

FIFA Standards & Damping

A FIFA-approved match ball dropped from 2 m onto steel must rebound between 1.35 m and 1.55 m, fixing e ∈ [0.82; 0.88]. With human foot mass (m₁ ≈ 1.2 kg) and ball mass (m₂ = 450 g), the theoretical ratio drops to v'₂/v₁ ≈ 1.34, and ≈ 1.2 in match conditions due to ankle flexion and footwear compliance.

Biomechanics

Segmental Inertia & Speed

A toe-kick mobilizes only the shank from the knee (lower moment of inertia, execution in 0.21 s). An inside-foot strike swings the entire lower limb from the hip (double the inertia, 0.42 s). Under defensive pressure, the toe-kick allows achieving higher foot speed v₁ in restricted time.

Motor control

Power vs Precision Tradeoff

While contact area does not alter speed, it dictates angular control: the broad surface of the inside of the foot forgives misalignment errors relative to the ball's center of mass, explaining its dominance for accurate passing and clinical finishing.
